# Digital Citizenship: Teaching Students to Be Safe Online

Being a "citizen" means having rights and responsibilities. The digital world is no different. Digital Citizenship is the norms of appropriate, responsible behavior with technology.

It goes beyond "safety" (don't get hacked). It covers "ethics" (don't be a jerk).

## The 9 Elements of Digital Citizenship

According to Mike Ribble (a leading expert), there are 9 key themes. Here are the top 4 most relevant for classrooms:

### 1. Digital Etiquette
*   **The Concept:** Electronic standards of conduct.
*   **The Lesson:** "Netiquette." Using proper grammar in emails to teachers. Not typing in ALL CAPS (shouting). Not spamming group chats.

### 2. Digital Law (Copyright)
*   **The Concept:** Responsibility for actions and deeds.
*   **The Lesson:** Plagiarism and Piracy. Teaching students that they cannot just "Right Click > Save As" any image on Google and put it in their presentation. Teach them about Creative Commons and citing sources.

### 3. Digital Footprint (Reputation)
*   **The Concept:** Managing your online identity.
*   **The Lesson:** Reminding students that colleges and employers search social media. A "funny" post today could cost a scholarship tomorrow.

### 4. Digital Health & Wellness
*   **The Concept:** Physical and psychological well-being.
*   **The Lesson:** Screen time balance. Discussing "Tech Neck" (posture) and sleep disruption caused by blue light. Encouraging offline hobbies.

## Why This Matters
If we only teach students how to *use* technology (coding, typing), but not how to use it *wisely*, we create technically skilled but ethically immature adults.

## Practical Tip: The "Classroom Constitution"
At the start of the year, have the class write their own "Digital Constitution."
*   "We will respect each other in online forums."
*   "We will cite our sources."
*   "We will help each other troubleshoot."

When students co-create the rules, they are more likely to follow them.
