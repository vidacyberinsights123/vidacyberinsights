# Safe Browsing: Tips for Research and Online Learning

The internet is the greatest library in history, but it's also a minefield. When you are deep in research mode, frantically clicking links to find a source for your paper, it is easy to stumble into the dark corners of the web.

Malicious sites often mask themselves as legitimate educational resources or PDF repositories. Here is how to keep your device clean while you study.

## 1. Stick to Trusted Domains

When looking for academic sources, look at the URL ending (Top-Level Domain).
*   **Safest:** `.edu` (Educational institutions), `.gov` (Government), `.org` (Non-profits - generally safe, but verify).
*   **Exercise Caution:** `.com`, `.net`.
*   **High Risk:** `.xyz`, `.info`, `.biz`, `.download`. These cheap domains are frequently used by scammers.

## 2. Beware of "Free PDF" Sites

You find a textbook that costs $200 listed for free on a site you’ve never heard of. You click "Download."

**The Trap:**
Instead of a PDF, you download an `.exe` or `.zip` file. Or, the site asks you to "Update your PDF Reader" to view the file.
**The Reality:** These are almost always malware.
**Rule:** If a file claims to be a document but ends in `.exe`, `.scr`, or `.bat`, **delete it immediately**.

## 3. Use an Ad Blocker

Many "essay helper" or citation websites are riddled with malvertising (malicious advertising). These ads can sometimes infect your computer just by loading on the page.
*   **Recommendation:** Install a reputable ad blocker like **uBlock Origin** on your browser. It makes pages load faster and keeps you safer.

## 4. HTTPS is Non-Negotiable

Look for the padlock icon in the address bar.
*   **HTTP:** Data is sent in plain text. Any hacker on the network can see what you are reading or typing.
*   **HTTPS:** Data is encrypted.
*   **Tip:** Install the **HTTPS Only Mode** in your browser settings. It forces a secure connection or warns you if a site is insecure.

## 5. Be Careful with Extensions

Browser extensions can be great productivity boosters (grammar checkers, citation generators). But malicious extensions can read everything you type—including passwords.
*   **Audit:** Only install extensions from the official Chrome/Edge/Firefox Web Store.
*   **Check Permissions:** Does a "wallpaper changer" extension need "Access to data on all websites"? No. That is spyware.

## Conclusion

Research is about finding the truth. Don't let your quest for knowledge cost you your digital safety. Stick to reputable databases (like JSTOR or Google Scholar) and keep your skepticism high.
