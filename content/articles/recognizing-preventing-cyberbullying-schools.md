# Recognizing and Preventing Cyberbullying in Schools

Cyberbullying is often viewed as an "after school" problem. But when a student is harassed online at 8:00 PM, they bring that trauma into your classroom at 8:00 AM.

It affects attendance, focus, and school climate. As an educator, you are on the front lines of detection.

## Signs a Student is Being Cyberbullied
You might not see the texts, but you see the results.
*   **Withdrawal:** A formerly active student stops participating.
*   **Isolation:** A student who typically sits with a group is suddenly alone at lunch.
*   **Device Anxiety:** Being jumpy or visibly upset when checking their phone during breaks.
*   **Academic Decline:** Sudden drop in grades or homework completion.

## Creating a "Reporting-Safe" Environment
Students don't report bullying because they fear adults will overreact (banning phones) or underreact ("just ignore it").
*   **Anonymous Reporting:** Advocate for an anonymous "bullying box" or digital form where students can tip off administration.
*   **Validation:** Teach students that reporting isn't "snitching"—it's helping a friend in danger.

## Teaching Digital Empathy
Bullying often stems from a lack of empathy—it's easier to be cruel to a screen than a face.
*   **SEL (Social Emotional Learning):** Incorporate lessons on how online words hurt real people.
*   **The "Pause" Button:** Teach students to wait 5 minutes before posting a mean comment. Usually, the impulse fades.

## What to Do When You Suspect It
1.  **Document:** Note your observations (dates, times, behaviors).
2.  **Refer:** Follow your school's protocol. Usually, this means involving the counselor or vice principal.
3.  **Support:** Check in with the student. "I've noticed you seem a bit down lately. Is everything okay?" Knowing one adult cares can be a lifeline.

## Conclusion

Schools cannot police the internet, but they can support the students who live there. By building a culture of empathy and safety, you reduce the oxygen that cyberbullying needs to survive.
