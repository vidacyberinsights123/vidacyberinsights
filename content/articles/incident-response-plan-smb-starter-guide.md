# Creating an Incident Response Plan: A Starter Guide for SMBs

Imagine this: It's 9:00 AM on a Tuesday. Your receptionist calls you. "I can't open any files, and there is a message on my screen demanding Bitcoin."

What do you do?
*   Do you unplug the server?
*   Do you call the police?
*   Do you pay?

If you don't know the answer, you are already losing. An **Incident Response (IR) Plan** is a predetermined set of instructions to help your staff detect, respond to, and recover from network security incidents.

You don't need a 50-page binder. For an SMB, a simple 2-page checklist is better than nothing.

## Phase 1: Preparation (Before the Attack)

1.  **Identify the Team:** Who makes the decisions? Designate an "Incident Commander" (usually the owner or IT lead).
2.  **Key Contacts List:** Have a physical list (paper!) of phone numbers:
    *   IT Support / Managed Service Provider (MSP)
    *   Cyber Insurance Provider
    *   Legal Counsel
    *   Local Law Enforcement (Non-emergency line)
3.  **Backups:** Ensure your backups are running daily and, crucially, are **offline** or immutable. Ransomware can't encrypt a tape sitting in a safe.

## Phase 2: Detection and Analysis (Is it Real?)"

Not every computer glitch is a hack.
1.  **Verify:** Is IT just down, or is it malicious? Look for ransom notes, strange admin accounts, or impossible login times (e.g., login from Russia at 3 AM).
2.  **Assess Scope:** Is it one laptop? Or the entire server?

## Phase 3: Containment (Stop the Bleeding)

This is the most critical step to prevent spread.
1.  **Disconnect:** Pull the network cable or turn off the Wi-Fi on infected machines immediately. **Do NOT turn the machine off** unless absolutely necessary (you might destroy evidence needed for forensics).
2.  **Isolate:** Take the entire network offline if you suspect it is spreading.

## Phase 4: Eradication and Recovery (Get Back to Work)

1.  **Call the Pros:** This is where you call your MSP or Insurance Forensics team. Let them find the "Patient Zero" and remove the malware.
2.  **Restore:** Wipe the infected machines clean. Reinstall the OS. Then, restore data from your clean backups.
3.  **Reset:** Force a password reset for every user in the company.

## Phase 5: Post-Incident Activity (Learn)

Once the dust settles, hold a "Lessons Learned" meeting.
*   How did they get in?
*   Did our plan work?
*   What gaps do we need to close?

## Warning: To Pay or Not to Pay?

The FBI advises **against** paying ransoms.
1.  It encourages the criminals.
2.  There is no guarantee you will get your data back (they are criminals, after all).
3.  You might be added to a "sucker list" and attacked again.

A solid IR plan turns a catastrophe into an inconvenience. Build yours today.
