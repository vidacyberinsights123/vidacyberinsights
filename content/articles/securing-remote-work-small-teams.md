# Securing Remote Work: Best Practices for Small Teams

Remote work offers freedom and flexibility, but it also dissolves the traditional security perimeter. In the old days, you just had to secure the office firewall. Now, your company data lives on laptops in coffee shops, home offices, and maybe even on personal tablets.

For small teams without a dedicated IT department, this sprawl can be terrifying. But it is manageable. Here are the best practices for securing a remote workforce.

## 1. The Death of "Trust" (Zero Trust)

Adopt a "Zero Trust" mindset. This means: **Never trust, always verify.**
*   Don't assume a request is safe just because it comes from an employee's email (it could be hacked).
*   Don't assume a device is safe just because it's on the VPN.

## 2. Essential Tools for Remote Teams

You need a basic stack of security tools installed on *every* device that checks company email or files.
*   **Business-Grade Antivirus:** Free versions aren't enough. Use a managed EDR (Endpoint Detection and Response) solution that you can monitor centrally.
*   **VPN (Virtual Private Network):** Mandatory for anyone working from public Wi-Fi. It encrypts the connection back to your resources.
*   **Password Manager:** Teams/Enterprise versions allow you to securely share passwords (like the company Twitter login) without texting them to each other.

## 3. Handle BYOD (Bring Your Own Device) with Care

If employees use personal laptops for work, you have a problem. You can't control what software their kids install on it in the evening.
*   **Policy:** Ideally, issue company-owned laptops.
*   **If BYOD is necessary:** Requires a clear "Acceptable Use Policy" and consider using "Mobile Device Management" (MDM) software to enforce basic security (like requiring a lock screen pin).

## 4. Secure the Cloud

You probably use Office 365, Google Workspace, or Slack. These are cloud-based.
*   **MFA Everywhere:** Enable Multi-Factor Authentication on every single cloud service. This is responsible for blocking 99.9% of automated account hacks.
*   **Least Privilege:** Does the intern need admin access to the entire Dropbox? No. Give employees access *only* to the files they need to do their job.

## 5. The "Home Office" Audit

Encourage your team to secure their home environment.
*   Change default router passwords.
*   Don't let smart speakers (Alexa/Google Home) listen in on confidential Zoom calls.

## Summary

Remote work is here to stay. By implementing these practices, you ensure that your team can work from anywhere without bringing the security risks home with them.
