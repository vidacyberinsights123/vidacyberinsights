# Protecting Student Data: Best Practices for Teachers

Teachers are custodians of sensitive information: grades, medical notes, home addresses, and behavioral reports. In the age of EdTech, this data is often scattered across dozens of apps and platforms.

Protecting this data isn't just good practice; in many places (like the US with FERPA), it's the law. Here is how to keep your digital classroom secure.

## 1. Vet Your Apps (The "Free" Trap)
You found a cool new quiz app. It's free! But does it require students to log in with their full name and email? Where is that data stored?
*   **Rule:** Never use an app that hasn't been approved by your district's IT department. "Shadow IT" is a major source of leaks.

## 2. Secure Your Devices
teachers often carry laptops between home and school.
*   **Lock It:** Set your computer to auto-lock after 5 minutes of inactivity.
*   **Don't Share:** Do not let students use your "Teacher" computer to present. They might see an email notification or a grade book tab popping up.

## 3. Be Careful with Email
*   **PII Check:** Avoid emailing sensitive student info (like IEPs or grades) in plain text.
*   **The "To" Field:** When emailing a group of parents, always use **BCC** (Blind Carbon Copy). Putting everyone in the "CC" field exposes every parent's email address to the whole group.

## 4. Physical Security Still Matters
*   **Sticky Notes:** Do not write passwords on sticky notes attached to your monitor. Students notice everything.
*   **USB Drives:** If you use a USB drive for grading, encrypt it. If you lose it in the parking lot, anyone can read those files.

## 5. Account Hygiene
*   **MFA:** Enable Two-Factor Authentication on your Gradebook/LMS login.
*   **Log Out:** Always log out of shared computers in the staff lounge.

## Summary

Data privacy is part of student safety. Treating their digital records with the same care as their physical safety builds trust with parents and administration.
