# Public Wi-Fi Dangers: How to Stay Safe on Campus

Free Wi-Fi is a student staple. You connect at the library, the student union, the coffee shop, and the dorms. But public, open Wi-Fi networks are one of the easiest places for hackers to intercept your data.

Why is it dangerous? Because on an open network, you and the hacker are sitting in the same virtual room, shouting your traffic across the room.

## The "Man-in-the-Middle" Attack

Imagine you are passing a note to your friend. In a Man-in-the-Middle (MITM) attack, a hacker sits between you. You pass the note to the hacker (thinking it's the router), the hacker reads/edits it, and passes it to your friend. You never know they were there.

On public Wi-Fi, hackers can use software to "sniff" the airwaves and capture passwords, emails, and credit card numbers if they aren't properly encrypted.

## The "Evil Twin" Hotspot

You see a Wi-Fi network named "Campus_Coffee_Free." You connect. It works.
But actually, the real network is "Campus_Coffee_Guest."

You just connected to a hacker's laptop that is broadcasting a fake name. Now, everything you do flows through their computer. They can redirect you to fake banking sites or steal your session cookies.

## How to Protect Yourself

You don't have to stop using Wi-Fi, but you need to armor up.

### 1. The Ultimate Shield: A VPN
A **Virtual Private Network (VPN)** creates a secure, encrypted tunnel between your device and the internet.
*   Even if a hacker intercepts your data, it looks like gibberish code.
*   **Action:** Subscribe to a reputable VPN service (like NordVPN, ProtonVPN, or Mullvad). Avoid "Free VPNs" as they often sell your data to pay for the service.

### 2. Verify the Network Name
Ask the staff what the official Wi-Fi name is. Don't guess. If you see two networks with similar names, ask which one is real.

### 3. Disable "Auto-Connect"
Your phone constantly shouts "Is 'Starbucks Wi-Fi' here?" to every network it sees. Hackers can answer "Yes, I am!" and fool your phone.
*   **Action:** Go to settings and "Forget" public networks after you use them, or turn off "Auto-Join" for open networks.

### 4. Use Cellular Data for Sensitive Tasks
If you are about to check your bank balance or enter your social security number, turn off Wi-Fi. Use your phone's 4G/5G connection. It is significantly harder to hack than a coffee shop router.

## Summary

*   **Treat public Wi-Fi as hostile territory.**
*   **Use a VPN whenever possible.**
*   **Save the sensitive banking for home.**

Study hard, browse safe!
