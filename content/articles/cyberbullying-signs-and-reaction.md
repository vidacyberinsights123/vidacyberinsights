# Cyberbullying: What to Look For and How to React

Decades ago, if a child was bullied at school, home was a safe haven. Today, that is no longer true. Through social media and messaging apps, bullying can be 24/7, invasive, and relentlessly public.

Cyberbullying is the use of digital communication to harass, threaten, or humiliate someone. Because it happens on screens, parents often miss it until the damage is deep.

## The Signs: Is Your Child Being Targeted?

Kids rarely ask for help. They fear losing their devices or making the bullying worse. Watch for these behavioral changes:
1.  **Emotional Reactions:** Getting visibly upset, angry, or withdrawn after using their phone or computer.
2.  **Secrecy:** Hiding the screen when you walk by, or being jumpy when a notification pings.
3.  **Avoidance:** Suddenly not wanting to go to school or quitting social activities.
4.  **Sleep/Appetite Changes:** Classic signs of anxiety and depression.

## How to React: A 4-Step Action Plan

If you discover your child is being bullied online, your instinct might be to call the other parent or ban the internet. Pause. Take a breath.

### 1. Listen and Support (Don't Blame)
Do not say: *"Why did you post that?"* or *"Just delete the app."*
This feels like punishment to the child. Instead, say: *"I'm so sorry this is happening. It's not your fault. We will fix this together."*

### 2. Do Not Respond
Retaliating or responding to the bully gives them exactly what they want: a reaction. It can also escalate the situation.

### 3. Save the Evidence (Screenshots)
Before you block or report, you need proof.
*   Take screenshots of every mean text, comment, or post.
*   Copy the URL of the bully's profile.
If the bullying escalates to threats of violence, you will need this for the school or police.

### 4. Report and Block
*   **On the Platform:** Use the "Report" function on Instagram/TikTok/Snapchat. They have teams dedicated to harassment.
*   **Block:** Block the user so they can no longer contact your child.
*   **To the School:** If the bully is a classmate, report it to the school counselor or principal. Most schools have anti-bullying policies that cover off-campus cyberbullying if it impacts the learning environment.

## Prevention: The "Upstander" Mentality

Teach your children not just to be victims or bystanders, but **Upstanders**.
If they see a friend being bullied:
*   Don't like or share the mean post.
*   Reach out to the victim privately: *"Hey, I saw that. It wasn't cool. Are you okay?"*

Cyberbullying thrives in silence. Breaking that silence is the best defense.
