# The Top 5 Cybersecurity Threats Facing Small Businesses in 2024

A common myth among small business owners is: *"I'm just a local coffee shop/consultant/shop. Hackers don't care about me. They want the big banks."*

The reality is starkly different. 43% of cyber attacks target small businesses. Why? Because big banks have billion-dollar defense budgets. You likely don't. To a hacker, an SMB is low-hanging fruit—easy to break into and willing to pay to get their data back.

Here are the top 5 threats looming over the SMB landscape in 2024.

## 1. Ransomware

Ransomware is malicious software that encrypts your files—locking you out of your own data. The attacker then demands a payment (ransom) to restore access.
*   **Impact:** It’s not just the ransom cost. It’s the downtime. Can your business survive 3 weeks without access to customer records, billing, or email? Most can't.
*   **Defense:** Robust, offline backups are the only 100% cure.

## 2. Phishing & Social Engineering

Human error remains the #1 cause of breaches.
*   **The Threat:** An employee clicks a link in a fake invoice email, unknowingly downloading malware or handing over login credentials.
*   **Evolution:** AI tools are making phishing emails look perfect—no more typos or bad grammar.

## 3. Business Email Compromise (BEC)

This is a specific, high-value type of phishing.
*   **The Scenario:** A hacker compromises the email account of your CEO or a vendor. They send an email to your finance person: *"Hey, the vendor changed banks. Please wire the $20,000 payment to this new account number."*
*   **The Result:** The money is gone instantly, and insurance often doesn't cover it because it was "authorized" by an employee.

## 4. Insider Threats

Not every attacker is a stranger in a hoodie.
*   **Malicious Insider:** A disgruntled employee who copies client lists before quitting.
*   **Accidental Insider:** A well-meaning employee who sets their password to "123456" or leaves a laptop unlocked at a coffee shop.

## 5. Supply Chain Attacks

You might be secure, but what about your vendors?
*   **The Chain:** Hackers breach a small HVAC contractor to get the credentials to attack the large Target store they service (this literally happened).
*   **For SMBs:** Conversely, if you use a software provider for your payroll, and *they* get hacked, your data is exposed.

## Conclusion

Ignorance is not bliss; it’s a liability. By understanding the landscape, you can direct your limited resources to the defenses that matter most: backups, training, and multi-factor authentication.
